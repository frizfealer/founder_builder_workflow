F1 | Lies | Important | rules.md R-10, rules.md "What the agent does" row "the refund call returned a failed refund or answered that the order is not cancelled…", policy.md A17
Quote: R-10: "After an error the order system will not recover from, or an answer that the order is not cancelled, the agent sends no second refund call." Row: "the refund call returned a failed refund or answered that the order is not cancelled, and no refund call timed out | asks staff to issue the refund by the manual refund process". policy.md A17: "agent tells the customer: the order is cancelled but the refund was not issued; staff will follow up"
Evidence: Question 16 (stand-in accepted): "It sends no second refund call, tells the customer the refund was not issued, and asks staff to follow up with the reason refund not issued." It says nothing about telling the customer the order is cancelled, or about asking staff to issue a manual refund. The spec's own D-14 reason says "A person must settle why the cancelled order is not cancelled in the order system." On this path the order system's latest answer is that the order is NOT cancelled. Even so, the agent tells the customer "the order is cancelled" and asks staff to refund it by hand. That goes against R-07 ("The agent issues the refund only after the order system has answered… that the order is cancelled") and C-38 ("The agent's last message matches the final refund status"). model.py confirms it: refund_last "not_cancelled" goes to staff "not_issued", which says "the refund was not issued; issue it by the manual refund process", with the customer message "the order is cancelled but the refund was not issued".
Pattern: one outcome is folded into another outcome's handling (OrderNotCancelled reuses the failed-refund path). The customer message and the staff request then assert a state that the last tool answer contradicts.

F2 | Lies | Important | claims.md C-52, claims.md C-17, rules.md D-09 (and R-13, which depends on C-52)
Quote: C-52: "A staff task can also give the reason refund amount differs. | … | proposed, accepted | Yes, these four tools, with these answers and the added reason "refund amount differs"." D-09: "…and create a staff follow-up task, with the added reason refund amount differs. | the owner, accepting the recommendation |"
Evidence: Question 10's reply is a record, not an acceptance of the recommendation. It quotes the set shown to the user, with three reasons: "request_staff_followup(order_number, reason) with reasons refund_not_issued, refund_status_unknown, cancellation_unknown". It then says "Confirmed, with four changes", and none of the four changes adds a reason. The quote in C-52 is the interviewer's recommended answer, not the owner's reply. C-17, which does quote the owner, says a task names "one reason: refund not issued, refund status unknown, or cancellation unknown", so C-17 and C-52 disagree. D-09 also leaves out the owner's four changes and logs the decision as "accepting the recommendation". Question 8 (stand-in) supports "creates a staff task", but not a new reason value in the queue's contract. An agent built from R-13 would call the queue with a reason the owner's confirmed tool does not take.
Pattern: a claim's quote is the interviewer's recommended answer, not the owner's reply. The decision is then logged as the owner accepting a recommendation that the owner answered with their own, different record.

F4 | Lies | Nit | rules.md R-15 (second sentence), claims.md C-08, claims.md C-43, rules.md "What the agent does" row "the order list read failed with an error"
Quote: R-15: "When the read of the customer's orders fails with an error at the start of the conversation, the agent changes nothing and tells the customer it cannot see their orders right now." C-08: "A read of the customer's orders answers with the orders, times out, or fails with an error that is not a timeout. | system | order system | spec only |". C-43: "| missing | | nobody |"
Evidence: Question 10 offered list_orders with two outcomes only: "returns each of the signed-in customer's orders… or gets no answer". The words "tells the customer it cannot see their orders right now" were the interviewer's proposal for the no-answer case. The owner replaced that case with "Reads may be retried on timeout without limit" (Question 10, change 4; restated in Questions 11 and 14). No answer gives list_orders a non-timeout error outcome. The spec marks the claim "spec only" and the behaviour "missing / nobody", yet R-15 states a behaviour for it. D-11, the decision R-15 rests on, covers only timeouts, so no decision is logged for this sentence.
Pattern: a rule supplies behaviour for a case that its own claim marks missing and that nobody decided, and reuses wording the owner had overridden.

F5 | Gap | Important | rules.md R-15, claims.md C-44, rules.md D-11, rules.md "What the agent does" row "a read timed out | reads again"
Quote: R-15: "A read that times out may be retried without limit; only calls that change something are bounded." Row: "a read timed out | reads again | R-15". C-44, column "what ends it": "nothing the owner named; the read answering, failing with an error, or the customer leaving"
Evidence: Question 14 asked directly: "the order list call times out again and again while the customer waits. What ends it?" The reply restates the Question 10 line ("Reads may be retried on timeout without limit; only commit calls are bounded.") and does not answer what ends it. The spec records that as decided (D-11, "the owner"). Nothing ends the loop, and nothing tells the agent what to say to a waiting customer. This covers the first order-list read, the re-read after a cancel timeout, and the re-read after a refund timeout (R-08 and R-11 both depend on a re-read that may never return).
Pattern: a repeated call with no end. The owner restated the premise rather than answering the question, so it should go back to the owner as an open question. It should not be logged as a decision, and the reviewer should not invent a bound.

F6 | Unclear | Nit | rules.md R-12, rules.md "What the agent does" rows "…says the order is cancelled and the refund was made, with its amount, payment method and status", policy.md A15 and A17
Quote: R-12: "it says a refund was made only when the order system showed a refund with status pending or issued for the amount the customer said yes to, and gives that status."
Evidence: the owner keeps pending and issued apart (Question 4: "Refund status: pending, issued, failed"). The owner requires that the agent "does not claim success" (Question 9, case 2) and that the "Final message must match the final refund status" (Question 9, case 3). C-21, which R-12 rests on, says an accepted refund "can still fail later". The Stripe docs read add that a refund can be held "as pending… until your Stripe balance becomes sufficient". "Made" is not defined. The owner could read "the refund was made" as "issued", even though the spec uses it for a pending refund as well.
Pattern: a customer-facing word coined to cover two owner-distinguished statuses, with no definition.

F7 | Gap | Important | rules.md R-05, R-16, rules.md "What the agent does"
Quote: absent. The nearest lines are R-05, "When the customer changes any part of the proposal after saying yes, such as naming a different order…", the row "the customer said yes and named a different open order | states the changed proposal…", and R-16, "one order at a time, each with its own proposal and its own yes".
Evidence: Question 7 (stand-in accepted) lets the customer cancel several orders, one at a time. The cases an agent will meet with no row:
(a) two orders named in one message ("cancel HG-1 and HG-2"). scenarios.md SC-42 cites R-16, R-04 and R-02, but no row says which order goes first or that the second waits.
(b) a second order named while a proposal waits, before any yes ("also cancel HG-2"). R-05 covers only changes after a yes, so the agent cannot tell whether this replaces the proposal or is a second request.
(c) a request after the first order is finished. chart.mmd puts "Cancelled and refunded, customer told" and "Handed to staff, customer told" under "Conversation over", and SC-41 says "the model holds one order per run", so no path or row continues the conversation.
(d) a customer asking for part of an order, store credit or a different card. R-06 states the rule, but no row says what the agent tells the customer (Question 3: "full-order cancellation only (no partial)… No cancellation fee, no store-credit option").
(e) a customer who says "cancel my order" and has no open order, or several orders none of them open. R-02 only covers more than one open order.
Pattern: R-16 and R-06 state a principle, but "What the agent does" has no row for the multi-request and out-of-policy-request cases the agent will meet.

F8 | Gap | Nit | rules.md R-12, R-13, rules.md "What the agent does" row "the refund call returned a refund, pending or issued, for the amount the customer said yes to… | says the order is cancelled and the refund was made, with its amount, payment method and status"
Quote: absent. R-13 covers only "an amount that differs from the amount the customer said yes to".
Evidence: the customer says yes to the payment method as part of the proposal (Question 5: "which order, the refund amount and where it goes"; R-04). The refund record carries its own payment method (Question 4: "Refund: refund id…, order number, amount, payment method, status"; C-06). The agent passes that method on to the customer, but no rule says what happens when it differs from the one confirmed. C-19 (Stripe) says the provider refunds only to the original method, but the value the agent reads comes from the order system's refund record.
Pattern: a confirmed value is passed on from a tool, and nothing is said about a mismatch. R-13 handles the amount but not the payment method.
