# Design influences

This skill is a synthesis, not a named industry-standard method.

It is influenced by:

- Simon Brown's emphasis on architecture drivers, a communicable architecture vision, risk, significance measured by the cost of change, just enough up-front design, and architecture as part of the delivery process.
- John Ousterhout's information hiding, deep modules, "Design It Twice", and avoiding unnecessary complexity.
- The Pragmatic Programmer's tracer-bullet approach: build a thin end-to-end slice to learn while keeping a coherent system skeleton.
- Mark Richards and Neal Ford, *Fundamentals of Software Architecture* (O'Reilly, 2020), chapters 4–8: architecture characteristics that need special structure, explicit versus implicit characteristics, translating domain concerns, concrete definitions of composite characteristics, and characteristics scoped to parts of a system. Chapter 19: decisions stated in an affirmative voice, justifications that explain why rather than how, and consequences that record negative impacts as well as positive ones.
- Architecture Decision Records for durable significant decisions, without turning every working design note into an ADR.
- Evolutionary architecture thinking: architecture changes when evidence creates design pressure rather than because a new iteration occurred.

The specific artifact shape — Scope / Drivers / Architecture Vision / Significant Decisions / Risks — and the tracer sufficiency stopping rule are conventions of this skill.
